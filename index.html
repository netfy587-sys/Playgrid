01The goal in one sentence

Find useful information on what to do: open every safe square without ever opening one that hides a mine. That is the whole puzzle.

Click to open

Opening a safe square shows a number, or a blank area.

Never open a mine

Opening a hidden mine ends the round immediately.

Open all safe squares

When every safe square is open, the puzzle is solved.

02Reading the numbers

A number counts the mines in the eight squares that touch it: up, down, left, right and the four corners.

This “3” touches three mines

Look at the picture: the centre number is 3 because exactly three of its eight neighbours hide a mine. The other five neighbours are safe. Small clues like this are all you need.

03Using flags to Make Informed Choices

A flag is only a note to yourself. It never changes the board, but it keeps your reasoning organised.

When a “1” has only one option

Every “1” on the left touches just one hidden square on the right. That square must be the mine, so you flag it, and the squares around it that are not touched by it can be opened with care.

04Why blank areas open all at once

A blank square means zero mines nearby, so the board safely opens its neighbours too, and keeps going outward.

A cascade of safe squares

The blanks spread like a wave until they reach squares with numbers. Those numbered squares form a border and give you your next clues.

06Smart habits that help

Playgrid keeps these tips short, so you can try them right away.

Start at the edges

Squares near walls and corners have fewer neighbours, so clues are easier.

Check the ones first

A “1” touching one hidden square is usually the quickest clue to solve.

Compare neighbours

Two numbers side by side often reveal which squares are safe.

Flag only what you know

A wrong flag can mislead you, so mark only certain mines.

Avoid rushing

Slow, careful clicks beat fast ones.

Take breaks

Rest your eyes, and enjoy puzzles in moderation.

08Quick answers

Get the information you need in one place.

Is it only luck?

Mostly it is logic. Rarely, a puzzle forces a guess, but most can be solved by careful reasoning.

Is my first click safe?

Most versions make sure your first click is safe, so you never lose on the very first move.

What does a number mean?

It tells you how many of the eight touching squares hide a mine. Nothing more, nothing less.

What is a flag for?

A flag is only your own reminder that a square hides a mine. It helps you keep track while you think.
